INTERPRETATION
--------------

1. Both models perform very strongly on the random held-out test set.
2. DistilBERT provides a modest improvement over TF-IDF on the random test set.
3. Cross-source performance is substantially worse than random held-out performance.
4. Ling -> SpamAssasin is particularly difficult for both models.
5. DistilBERT Ling -> SpamAssasin accuracy (0.4356) is below the majority-class baseline (0.7490), despite high positive recall (0.9533).
6. This indicates substantial source/distribution shift and demonstrates why random train/test evaluation alone is insufficient.
7. SpamAssasin -> Ling generalizes considerably better, with DistilBERT reaching 0.9184 accuracy and 0.7929 positive F1.
8. Feature analysis suggests corpus-specific vocabulary contributes to the observed generalization problem.
9. External Nazario evaluation gives the TF-IDF baseline a phishing recall of 0.8089.
10. The next evaluation step should compare DistilBERT against Nazario before moving to manipulation-signal engineering and robustness experiments.

LIMITATIONS
-----------

- The main supervised training task is broader malicious/spam email classification, not a pure phishing-only label.
- Ling and SpamAssasin have different corpus characteristics.
- Nazario contains only positive/phishing examples.
- Random held-out performance may be optimistic relative to real-world distribution shift.
- Cross-source results demonstrate meaningful generalization limitations.
